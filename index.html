Could this need a hospital right away?

  • Severe trouble breathing
  • Heart attack or cardiac arrest
  • Open fracture or internal injury
  • Serious burns
  • Likely surgery
  • Amputation or loss of an eye
  • Fatality or probable fatality

Call a chief now, before the report

The report can wait. The on duty chief has to notify L&I within 8 hours of a serious injury or hospitalization.

Minor events can be filed as a record only and turned into a workers' comp claim at any time.

Where on your body?

Left, right or both shows up for arms, hands, legs and feet.

Exposure or contamination?

Contamination is contact that doesn't meet exposure criteria: decontaminate and document it. Exposure is contact with blood, body fluids or airborne germs that can spread disease: it needs the Biological Exposure Procedure right away.

Exposure details

Flush skin or eyes with water 15 minutes or more, wash with soap and water, bag contaminated clothing or PPE, clean gear per agency SOP.

At the scene, before anything else

  1. Do not leave the scene.
  2. Call law enforcement to report it, every time, even with no damage you can see.
  3. Notify the on duty BC right away.
  4. Make sure the scene is safe and patient care is covered. Ask for more resources if you need them.
  5. Take photos on the agency phone only.
  6. Stick to facts. Don't admit fault, blame anyone or guess at the cause.
